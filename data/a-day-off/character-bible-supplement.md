# A Day Off — Supplementary AU Character Bible
Applies only to the separate **A Day Off** story workspace. This SUPPLEMENTS the pre-existing twelve-viewer Attack on Titan Character Bible; it never replaces that bible, imports another project's chapters, or rewrites the original cast.

## Continuity key
- **ESTABLISHED** = specifically confirmed for this AU.
- **PROPOSED** = usable writing direction, not automatically a fact of past history.
- **UNRESOLVED** = deliberately undecided by the author.

## FRANK GALLAGHER — Levi's father (ONSCREEN ONLY)
**ESTABLISHED:** Frank is an original crossover adaptation inspired by the *Shameless* character, not a literal Chicago transplant. He is Levi's father in this AU. He exists in the projected OVA's world, **never physically in the watching room**. There is an affectionate, lived-in relationship between him and Levi even when it is unreliable or painful.

### Psychological engine
Frank is clever, widely read or self-educated in this AU, verbally agile, funny, and skilled at sizing up an audience. He is capable of explaining politics, class hypocrisy, military spending and human selfishness with uncomfortable precision. He deploys that intelligence less to improve his life than to justify not changing it. When cornered by consequences, he converts shame into a monologue about freedom, corrupt institutions or other people's hypocrisy. Sometimes the argument contains a real insight; it still evades the immediate question.

Alcohol use, chronic avoidance, poverty, and self-sabotage shape his behavior; they do not erase choice or accountability. He can be sober enough to see exactly what he has done and still fail to repair it. His grand theories often disguise an immediate and mundane need: another drink, shelter, an unpaid debt, somebody to take responsibility. Do not give him clinical diagnoses as settled fact, describe him as incapable of love, or make him an omniscient mastermind. The prompt's analyses of narcissism/antisocial traits are *interpretive lenses*, not medical labels. Frank's intelligence is a talent squandered through everyday decisions, not proof that every injury was calculated.

He calls dependence on others an act of resistance against a corrupt world. Underneath, he fears abandonment, ordinary responsibility, accountability, and discovering that even his best effort may not be enough. His humor and rhetorical brilliance should sometimes win the audience over **before** revealing what he actually did.

### Frank as a father
**ESTABLISHED:** Frank genuinely loves Levi, misses him, can feel pride in his rank, remembers personal details, enjoys having him near, and sometimes tries to keep him safe. Levi loves Frank back. They share humor, familiarity, affectionate banter and moments of real comfort. This cannot be written as a purely one-sided obligation, a one-note abuser story, or a convenient fraud subplot.

Frank also sees Levi's salary, military rank, competence and connections as available family resources. The shift from “my son came to see me” to “my son can fix this” can happen over one conversation, without Frank recognizing how much he has just asked. He may borrow against Levi's name, imply approval he does not have, or promise an introduction. His claim that hardship made Levi strong turns a true observation into a selfish justification; Frank has not earned credit for what Levi endured.

**PROPOSED recognizable rhythms:** Frank throws out a ridiculous remark; Levi answers without missing a beat. Frank notices an old habit or injury that strangers miss. They share a meal or a joke that has nothing to do with debt. Frank proudly tells a stranger Levi is a captain, then uses that same fact to pressure a creditor. Frank slips into wounded outrage when Levi refuses and, later, attempts reconciliation with a trivial, oddly attentive gesture. The gesture may be sincere and still insufficient.

Frank is not always lying. Sometimes he believes the excuse as he is making it. Sometimes he manipulates consciously. Sometimes he is generous at the exact wrong moment. He may offer his son the last of something he needs, then steal from him the next day. The contradiction is his character.

### Levi's side of the relationship
Levi does not become a helpless, permanently angry victim around Frank. He can genuinely look forward to a visit, arrive with necessities and decide to stay to talk. He recognizes scams faster than most people, often predicts his father's deflections, and sets limits. His irritation does not cancel his affection; his affection does not mean consent. Let him laugh, choose, negotiate, leave, return, refuse and revise those decisions himself. He is not waiting for Erwin to rescue him or tell him how to feel.

**PROPOSED:** Levi knows some version of Frank's routine well enough to recognize when the jokes are avoiding a real problem. Frank may know a concrete private habit from Levi's childhood; **which habit and how he learned it remain author decisions**, not license to invent a definitive Kuchel backstory.

### Dialogue and performance
Frank speaks in energetic, digressive, theatrical stretches with moments of surprising acuity. He can shift from affectionate teasing to polemic to bargaining to self-pity in minutes. He swears and jokes when appropriate to the setting. Do not make every line a speech. Ordinary back-and-forth should be easy and funny. Do not have him constantly announce that he loves Levi, or speak like a psychologist explaining his own pathology.

A model interaction: Frank notices Levi came with bread and fresh socks, thanks him affectionately, immediately complains about military salaries, offers Levi a seat, and only later tries to raise the “tiny favor” he has already promised someone.

### The father-son arc
Do not resolve this in one confrontation, apology, sacrifice, or deathbed insight. Frank can improve a little, regress, surprise Levi, and make a boundary necessary again. The reader should be able to laugh with him, recoil from his choices, and understand why Levi still answers the door.

### Do not transplant modern Shameless backstory as AU canon
University of Northwestern, Chicago institutions, contemporary drugs/social security, Monica, Peggy, Fiona, Lip, Ian, Debbie, Carl, Liam, all their precise histories, and Frank's television-series ending **are not established in this AU**. Their structural ideas (inverted family roles, inherited trauma, social performance, predatory access to family resources, wasted talent) may inspire grounded fantasy-setting equivalents, but never silently introduce those characters, modern institutions or events.

## NILE DOK / DAWK — Military Police officer (ONSCREEN ONLY)
**ESTABLISHED:** Nile is an adult, institutionally experienced Military Police officer and a father. He and Erwin share a past and significant philosophical friction. Nile works independently with Erwin on suspicious supply/requisition paperwork in this OVA; **he does not physically join the watching room**.

### Personal engine
Nile is practical, cautious, dry, protective of civilians and profoundly invested in a safe domestic life. He is not a simple coward or humorless bureaucrat. He believes continuity and verifiable process can protect ordinary people from chaos, even when institutions are compromised. That belief is partly honorable and partly convenient. Erwin is the road he did not take: a friend/rival who sacrificed stability for an unsettling ideal. Nile's irritation at Erwin can be intellectual, moral, familiar, or uncomfortably personal, never just cartoonish grumbling.

Marie and their family matter to him as people, not a prize he “won” or proof that his choices were morally superior. **PROPOSED internal tension:** Nile sometimes needs Erwin's reckless-seeming choice to be wrong so that the sacrifices of his own path continue to make sense. This should emerge through decisions and conversation, not expository diagnosis.

He understands that quietly tolerated corruption is not the same as justice. His most important boundary is harm to civilians and children. He may cooperate with a flawed system while working to prevent indiscriminate harm from it. Do not import his much later fate, the truth about Titans, future coups, or future knowledge into his thoughts during the OVA.

### Investigative competence
Nile insists on dates, authorizations, actual witnesses, chains of custody and distinguishing clerical error from intentional wrongdoing. The file in question includes a payment processed before its requisition was filed, a missing/irregular authorization, a clerk called Brenner and Voss Supply and Transport. Nile is not stupid for refusing a conspiracy theory on incomplete evidence. Erwin is not automatically correct because he asks a wider question. Make them disagree productively and each catch things the other missed.

### Dialogue
Nile speaks with military specificity, dry exasperation, an occasional barb based on longstanding familiarity with Erwin, and rare vulnerability. More clipped than Frank, less theatrical than Hange, not a stoic caricature. At times Nile is funny *on purpose*. Allow brief talk about ordinary life that has nothing to do with the mystery.

## ERWIN + LEVI — restrained, lived-in Eruri
**ESTABLISHED:** They are in love here. Erwin knows and worries that his command and professional position might influence Levi's freedom to respond; Levi's understanding of his feelings may be less neatly categorized. Their love is not an exchange of debts, a constant sequence of near-confessions, public possessiveness or jealousy.

**ESTABLISHED gestures:** Erwin arranged Levi's promotion to captain **with the associated salary adjustment**; bought a black horse appropriate for Levi's travel; gifted custom-fitted expensive leather gloves with reinforced palms after noting the old pair's condition; and quietly arranged other needed equipment/replacements. Treat the items as recurring practical realities, not a ledger or obligatory symbolic close-up. Levi accepts some care, objects to some, and makes his own choices. Erwin notices habits, equipment, tiredness and preferences without turning Levi into a project.

Their friendship contains banter, trust, occasional friction and practical joking. Their conversations need not be cryptic. Erwin should never automatically take over Levi's interactions with Frank. It is possible for Erwin to understand the difficulty, offer help, and let Levi decline.

## Kuchel — UNRESOLVED
Levi's mother Kuchel: alive, absent, deceased, whereabouts, relationship to Frank and events of Levi's childhood remain undecided. DO NOT imply a final answer or reveal it in the opening installment.

## Knowledge and boundaries
The OVA takes place months before Eren joins the military, before major Titan revelations. The **watching room is later** (approximately two days before the 57th Expedition). Screen chronology and room chronology are different. Spectators may know things from their own lives at room time but do not know the major later spoilers. They do not automatically share one another's private history.

The original twelve AOT reaction-room character profiles and their established personal voices remain the foundation. Frank and Nile are **onscreen characters with active character sheets**, not viewers. Their inclusion in canon context is **not permission to seat them in the room**.

## Style fail-safes
Write psychologically layered character-driven fiction, not therapy commentary. Avoid pathologizing dialogue, speeches about trauma that explain too much, melodrama, tidy emotional closure, endless almost-confessions, turning Nile into comic exasperation, or making Frank a mere clown/villain. Every scene needs characters pursuing concrete goals. Let jokes and care precede complications so their loss has weight.
