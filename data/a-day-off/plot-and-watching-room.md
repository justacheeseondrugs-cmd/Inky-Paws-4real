# A Day Off — OVA Plot and Watching-Room Execution
**Status:** an outline and set of working constraints, **not an already-written chapter or finalized continuity**.
**Premise (ESTABLISHED):** On an ordinary day a couple of months before Eren joins the military, Levi visits his father Frank. Frank has promised something leveraging Levi's name/rank/contacts without permission while struggling with money. Independently, Erwin and Nile are studying discrepancies in military supply payments. Their paths intersect at Voss Supply and Transport.

## Central question
What does Levi owe a father he loves but cannot reliably trust, while a mundane favor begins to pull the family into an investigation neither father nor son controls? A parallel investigative question: is the early payment and irregular authorization fraud, negligence, or a more complicated administrative problem?

## Two equal storylines
1. **Levi and Frank:** Levi brings food/supplies and actually wants to be there. Start with affectionate familiarity and jokes. Show Frank's genuine pride, attention and selfish shortcuts before introducing the precise promise he made. Escalate through a debt, an unwanted meeting, and friction over consent and rank. A boundary should change the way they next speak without solving everything. Frank may love Levi, hurt him, feel shame, defend himself, and later perform a small genuine gesture.
2. **Erwin and Nile:** Follow records, requisition dates and missing paperwork. Brenner's authorization is irregular or unaccounted for; Voss Supply and Transport appears in a payment that seems to precede the required requisition. Let Nile insist on testimony and primary records rather than accusatory speculation, while Erwin asks who benefits. They have equal investigative agency, including wrong hypotheses and useful corrections.

## Intersection
Frank's short-term maneuver inadvertently leads Levi to a Voss-connected person or transaction. Erwin and Nile reach the same lead independently. Frank is not behind the whole military affair, not an all-knowing informant, and not responsible for arranging a convenient grand conspiracy. The crossing creates funny timing, embarrassment, credible professional risk and a piece of information neither storyline could collect alone.

## Proposed six-part OVA mini-series (PROPOSED; adjustable)
1. **A Day Off:** Levi's ordinary errands and eagerness to see Frank; warmth and sharp dialogue. Elsewhere Erwin and Nile notice the dating mismatch and missing authorization. Cliffhanger: a name/claim linking Frank's arrangement to Voss.
2. **A Familiar Face:** A fuller father-son afternoon, a real shared joke and an intimate memory; a visitor reveals a promise Frank made. Nile checks who entered the payment and when.
3. **Unpaid Debts:** Levi refuses to ratify Frank's promise. Frank tries charm, outrage and self-pity; affection remains. Erwin finds that Voss's records are plausible in parts and inconsistent in others. Brenner cannot be assumed guilty.
4. **An Unfortunate Coincidence:** The Voss encounter collides with the independent inquiry. Erwin, Nile, Levi and Frank each act for their own reasons. Comedy gives way to a choice with material consequences.
5. **The Things We Owe:** Levi chooses a specific boundary and a practical action that is *his*. Frank's attempt at reconciliation is sincere but not enough to erase harm. Nile and Erwin test competing explanations and gather corroboration.
6. **Not Quite a Day Off:** A partial documentary/investigative result and a changed arrangement between father and son, with no instant transformation. A wider irregularity, unanswered authorization question, or unresolved Voss beneficiary provides the next-OVA hook.

Do not force these six chapters to fit the same in-world day; subsequent installments may span multiple days. Do not force all mysteries to resolve together or elevate the fraud subplot above the father-son relationship.

## Watching room: audience vs onscreen
**ESTABLISHED for this workspace:** 12 existing Attack on Titan viewers, from the base character bible. **Frank Gallagher and Nile Dok are NEVER in the watching room.** They appear only in the projected story (Nile as needed during investigation). This is not a crossover in which Frank sits beside the cadets. Do not add Joseph, Kael, Anya, Candy, a narrator-avatar or other projects' characters by default.

Show reactions *during* screened events in substantial scenes; do not dump reaction summaries after each OVA segment. A few characters carry a conversational beat, others remain silent. Some notice Erwin's gloves/horse practical gifts, or Frank's shifts, before others. Interpretations evolve: laughter at Frank -> recognition of genuine closeness -> disbelief -> discomfort without unanimous agreement. Some mistakenly read Levi's patience as naive, or Erwin's attention as ordinary command responsibility, or Nile's caution as cowardice. Let watchers challenge each other instead of delivering a moral diagnosis.

Avoid the roll-call pattern where every viewer comments, instant shipper commentary for every Eruri gesture, and premature consensus. The room does not know Annie/Reiner/Bertolt's identities, the outside world, the Reiss revelation, or future Titan-related outcomes unless genuinely revealed in story time. The watchers do not know details of Levi's private family life merely because the writer does.

## Eruri throughout
The undercurrent is practical: care expressed via gloves, horse, repairs, knowledge of habits, consideration without condescension, trust amid disagreement. Erwin should not resolve every Levi/Frank beat. Erwin and Nile's investigation has independent causal logic; they do not exist just to be in a room with Levi.

## Open questions (UNRESOLVED)

- What exactly Frank promised Voss, for how much, and what he believed when he said it.
- Whether Brenner made a mistake, concealed evidence, or was used.
- Whether the early payment is fraud, bureaucracy, or a hybrid.
- How much Erwin already suspects at the outset.
- When Levi learns specific facts about Frank's deal.
- The precise OVA count, detailed chapter endings and next installments.
No single suggested answer becomes canon until explicitly chosen by the author.

## Reader-facing craft test
Every installment should contain something delightful that explains why Levi returns to Frank, something Frank does that complicates that affection, one concrete investigative advance or correction, and distinct reactions that change how the audience thinks. The style is lively, natural prose with consequential action, not clinical analysis disguised as dialogue.
