ZEKE YEAGER — CHARACTER
ANALYSIS & WRITING REFERENCE
Attack on Titan | Canon Character Study + Your Name AU

1. CORE CHARACTERIZATION
Zeke Yeager is an intelligent, calculating, deeply ideological man whose personality is
shaped by childhood trauma, emotional detachment, intellectual superiority, and a
profoundly distorted understanding of human suffering.

He is not particularly interested in being liked. He is interested in understanding, predicting,
and controlling the people and circumstances around him.

He possesses an unusual combination of qualities:

   ●​   Exceptional intelligence and analytical ability.
   ●​   A detached, sometimes almost clinical approach to human life.
   ●​   A dry, understated and occasionally absurd sense of humour.
   ●​   Considerable patience and strategic thinking.
   ●​   Emotional defensiveness disguised as intellectual certainty.
   ●​   A capacity for attachment that he rarely allows himself to acknowledge.
   ●​   A tendency to intellectualize painful experiences rather than emotionally process
        them.

Zeke is neither an emotionally empty sociopath nor a misunderstood innocent. He is a man
who has developed an elaborate worldview that allows him to live with his own experiences
and actions.

His intelligence is genuine, his cruelty is real, and his contradictions are essential to his
characterization.

The central contradiction: Zeke is capable of recognizing suffering in others while
remaining remarkably detached from the individual human beings affected by his own
decisions.


2. CHILDHOOD AND FAMILY HISTORY
Zeke is the son of Grisha Yeager and Dina Fritz.

He grows up in Marley as an Eldian child, under a system that discriminates against Eldians
and confines them to internment zones.
His parents are involved in the Eldian Restorationist movement. They raise him with the
expectation that he will become an instrument of their political ambitions.

Grisha and Dina do not adequately recognize Zeke as an individual child with his own
needs. Instead, they see him as someone who must fulfill a historical and ideological
responsibility.

Zeke is subjected to an enormous amount of pressure, particularly through the Warrior
program.

His childhood is characterized by:

   ●​   Fear of disappointing his parents.
   ●​   The pressure to perform and succeed.
   ●​   Emotional isolation.
   ●​   The expectation that his value depends on his usefulness.
   ●​   A lack of reliable emotional safety.

His relationship with his parents is complicated. He wants their approval, but he also
experiences their expectations as an unbearable burden.

Tom Ksaver

Tom Ksaver becomes one of the most important relationships of Zeke's life.

Ksaver is not simply a mentor. He provides something Zeke has been missing: an adult who
takes an interest in him without treating him as an instrument of a political movement.

Their relationship is based on shared interests, conversation, intellectual curiosity and
mutual trust.

Ksaver introduces Zeke to baseball, which becomes a recurring part of Zeke's identity.

More importantly, Ksaver gives Zeke the opportunity to speak honestly about his
experiences.

Zeke's decision to report his parents to Marley is closely connected to Ksaver's influence
and his desire to escape the situation in which he has been placed.

This relationship is essential when writing Zeke. He is capable of forming meaningful
attachments, particularly when someone approaches him without demanding that he fulfill a
predetermined role.

However, his experience of attachment remains limited and complicated.


3. IDEOLOGY AND WORLDVIEW
Zeke's ideology is inseparable from his childhood experiences.
He comes to believe that Eldian existence itself is the source of an enormous amount of
suffering.

His euthanasia plan is based on the belief that preventing Eldians from reproducing would
eventually end their suffering and bring an end to the conflict associated with their existence.

He considers this a compassionate solution.

That does not make it compassionate in practice.

His worldview is fundamentally paternalistic: he believes he understands what is best for an
entire population, even when that means removing their ability to choose their own future.

Zeke frequently approaches moral questions through abstraction.

He can discuss the fate of entire populations without engaging with the individual
experiences of the people involved.

This is partly ideological and partly defensive.

He has learned to transform personal suffering into an intellectual problem. Doing so allows
him to maintain a sense of control.

His relationship with freedom

Zeke and Eren have fundamentally different ideas about freedom.

Eren's understanding of freedom is associated with the ability to live and act without external
restrictions.

Zeke's worldview is more pessimistic. He sees existence itself as something that can impose
suffering on people.

He does not necessarily believe that everyone deserves the opportunity to pursue their own
future.

This ideological contrast is particularly important in any story involving Eren, Armin and the
other characters.


4. INTELLIGENCE AND STRATEGIC THINKING
Zeke is one of the most intellectually capable characters in Attack on Titan.

His intelligence is not limited to military strategy.

He demonstrates:

    ●​ Excellent observational skills.
    ●​ An ability to identify weaknesses in other people's reasoning.
    ●​ Long-term strategic planning.
   ●​   Political awareness.
   ●​   Psychological manipulation.
   ●​   The ability to adapt when circumstances change.
   ●​   A tendency to anticipate several possible outcomes.

He is particularly good at maintaining an outward appearance of calm while evaluating a
situation.

Zeke rarely needs to dominate a conversation through volume or aggression.

He can allow another person to speak, observe their assumptions, and then use that
information to his advantage.

He is also capable of deliberately presenting himself as less threatening, more cooperative
or more approachable than he actually is.

Important writing distinction

Zeke should not be portrayed as omniscient.

He is intelligent, but his intelligence operates within the limits of his knowledge and
assumptions.

He can misunderstand people. He can underestimate emotional motivations. He can make
strategic mistakes, particularly when his ideology prevents him from recognizing alternative
perspectives.

He is particularly vulnerable when confronted with experiences that do not fit his established
worldview.


5. PERSONALITY AND SOCIAL BEHAVIOUR
Zeke is generally composed, articulate and conversational.

He does not have the explosive temperament of Eren or the intimidatingly blunt
communication style of Levi.

He often appears relaxed, even in situations where other people would be visibly distressed.

His conversational style can be surprisingly casual.

He enjoys intellectual exchanges, occasionally teases people and has a dry sense of
humour.

He is not constantly serious.

In fact, some of his most memorable moments involve his rather peculiar combination of
confidence, absurdity and intellectual detachment.
Humour

Zeke's humour tends to be:

   ●​   Dry.
   ●​   Understated.
   ●​   Occasionally sarcastic.
   ●​   Sometimes deliberately provocative.
   ●​   Occasionally childish or unexpectedly silly.

His interest in baseball is particularly useful for showing this less imposing side of his
personality.

He can be amused by relatively ordinary things.

He should not, however, become a constant source of jokes. His humour works best when it
emerges naturally from his observations and interactions.

Emotional expression

Zeke rarely displays vulnerability directly.

When uncomfortable, he may:

   ●​   Change the subject.
   ●​   Make an intellectual observation.
   ●​   Use humour to deflect attention.
   ●​   Become unusually quiet.
   ●​   Retreat into detached reasoning.

He is not incapable of affection or emotional attachment.

Rather, intimacy is unfamiliar territory, particularly when it requires him to acknowledge his
own emotional needs.


6. RELATIONSHIPS
Grisha Yeager

Their relationship is marked by disappointment, resentment and emotional neglect.

Zeke wants parental approval but ultimately feels that his parents value his political
usefulness more than his individuality.

His relationship with Grisha becomes especially important when considering his later
interactions with Eren.

Tom Ksaver
Ksaver represents one of the few genuinely nurturing relationships in Zeke's life.

He is an important example of the kind of connection Zeke can develop when he feels
understood rather than evaluated.

Eren Yeager

Zeke initially approaches Eren as a potential ally and, eventually, as someone with whom he
shares a particular family history.

He projects his own experiences onto Eren.

He assumes that Eren must have suffered under Grisha in the same way he did.

His inability to recognize Eren's independent motivations becomes one of his significant
mistakes.

Their relationship demonstrates Zeke's tendency to interpret other people's experiences
through his own psychological framework.

Pieck Finger

Pieck is one of the people who has known Zeke through his years as a Warrior and military
officer.

Their relationship has an established professional familiarity.

She is perceptive, intelligent and capable of recognizing inconsistencies in the behaviour of
people around her.

She should not be written as someone who blindly trusts Zeke.

Levi Ackerman

In canon, Levi and Zeke are enemies.

Their relationship is defined by military conflict, mutual hostility and Levi's personal
investment in stopping Zeke.

They are not friends, and Zeke is not someone Levi would naturally welcome into his
personal life.

This antagonism is important to preserve when adapting their relationship into an alternate
universe.


7. Zeke AND INTIMACY: AN IMPORTANT
PSYCHOLOGICAL DIMENSION
Zeke's romantic and intimate life is not extensively explored in canon.

We should therefore avoid making unsupported claims about his romantic history.

However, his established childhood experiences provide a useful foundation for exploring his
relationship with intimacy in an AU.

He grew up in an environment where affection was entangled with expectations, usefulness
and ideological responsibility.

Ksaver's mentorship offers a meaningful exception, but it does not establish that Zeke has
experienced a sustained, mutually intimate romantic relationship.

For this particular story, we can explore the possibility that Zeke has never experienced the
kind of everyday romantic affection that Erwin and Levi share.

This should be treated as an AU characterization choice, not an established canon fact.

What makes Erwin and Levi's relationship significant to him?

Zeke initially has no reason to understand their relationship.

He doesn't know Levi. He doesn't know Erwin. He doesn't know that the man whose body he
inhabits is romantically involved with someone.

His first experience of their relationship is physical and immediate.

He wakes up in Erwin's body, in an unfamiliar bed, with an unfamiliar man cuddling him.

The experience should initially produce confusion, caution and discomfort.

The emotional significance develops later.

Zeke gradually discovers that Levi's affection is not an isolated occurrence.

It is habitual.

Levi knows Erwin's preferences, routines, expressions and moods. He is comfortable
touching him. He trusts him. He expects him to be there.

Their intimacy is not necessarily dramatic or particularly demonstrative.

It is built into ordinary life.

For Zeke, this could become a profoundly unfamiliar experience to witness.

He is not simply discovering physical affection.

He is discovering what it looks like when someone knows another person thoroughly and
continues choosing them.
Important distinction

Zeke should not immediately fall in love with Levi.

His initial response should be confusion, followed by curiosity and gradually developing
emotional investment.

He may become attached to the experience of being treated with Erwin's familiarity before
he fully understands that the affection is not intended for him.

Eventually, he must confront the distinction between:

   ●​ Being loved by Levi.
   ●​ Being mistaken for someone Levi loves.
   ●​ Developing genuine feelings for Levi himself.

These are three different experiences.

The resulting emotional conflict should be complicated, uncomfortable and slow.


8. SPEECH PATTERNS AND DIALOGUE
Zeke's speech is generally articulate and controlled.

He tends to speak in complete thoughts and often approaches conversations as intellectual
exchanges.

He is comfortable explaining his reasoning and occasionally enjoys making other people
uncomfortable through pointed observations.

His dialogue should reflect his intelligence without becoming excessively formal.

Good dialogue characteristics

   ●​   Dry observations.
   ●​   Casual confidence.
   ●​   Occasional sarcasm.
   ●​   Thoughtful questions.
   ●​   Subtle provocations.
   ●​   Calm responses to hostility.
   ●​   Unexpectedly ordinary comments.

Avoid

   ●​   Making him constantly sarcastic.
   ●​   Giving him an exaggerated villainous laugh.
   ●​   Making every sentence sound like a philosophical lecture.
   ●​   Making him emotionally expressive without sufficient development.
   ●​ Turning him into a helpless, socially incompetent person when confronted with
      affection.

Zeke understands people reasonably well, even if he does not always understand their
emotional motivations.

He should be capable of reading a room.

He is not socially clueless.


9. PHYSICAL PRESENCE AND BODY LANGUAGE
Zeke is physically imposing.

He is tall, broad-shouldered and carries himself with considerable confidence.

His movements are generally controlled rather than frantic.

He does not need to constantly demonstrate his physical strength.

His body language often communicates a relaxed sense of control.

When uncomfortable, he may become more still rather than visibly agitated.

His expressions can be difficult to interpret, particularly when he is deliberately concealing
his thoughts.

In writing, avoid making him physically aggressive in every interaction.

His most interesting intimidation often comes from his composure.


10. IMPORTANT CONTRADICTIONS TO PRESERVE
Zeke should remain a character full of contradictions.

                      Trait                                      Contradiction

 Intelligence                                    Can be blinded by his own assumptions

 Compassion                                      Supports an ideology that denies others
                                                 reproductive freedom

 Emotional detachment                            Is capable of meaningful attachment

 Confidence                                      Has a deeply troubled relationship with his
                                                 own past

 Strategic thinking                              Can misinterpret emotional motivations
 Humour                                         Often uses it to avoid vulnerability

 Desire for peace                               Is willing to inflict enormous suffering to
                                                achieve it

 Curiosity                                      Can become dismissive when confronted
                                                with perspectives he rejects

These contradictions should not disappear as he develops relationships with other
characters.

His eventual friendships should complicate his worldview rather than magically resolve his
psychological difficulties.




11. YOUR NAME AU — SPECIFIC
CHARACTERIZATION RULES
This section establishes how canon Zeke should be adapted to the body-swapping story.

Initial circumstances

Zeke has never visited Paradis.

He has never met Erwin Smith.

He has never met Levi Ackerman.

He has no personal knowledge of the Survey Corps, its members, military structure or
internal relationships.

He knows about the island through Marleyan education, propaganda and military
information, but his knowledge is limited to what he could realistically have learned from
those sources.

He does not know what humanity inside the Walls looks like in everyday life.

He has never heard of Levi's reputation as Humanity's Strongest Soldier.

He does not know Erwin is in a relationship.

First body swap

Zeke wakes up in Erwin's body.
He finds himself in an unfamiliar bedroom, with an unfamiliar man sleeping beside him and
holding him.

He does not recognize Levi.

He does not understand their relationship.

He has no idea who Erwin is supposed to be.

His immediate priorities should be:

   1.​ Understand where he is.
   2.​ Determine who the people around him are.
   3.​ Avoid revealing that he is unfamiliar with his supposed identity.
   4.​ Identify possible threats.
   5.​ Gather information.

His intelligence should help him improvise, but he should make mistakes.

Returning to his own body

When Zeke wakes up in his own body, he immediately understands that someone else has
occupied it.

The same applies to Erwin.

They initially know nothing about one another.

Their first reactions should be suspicion, frustration and an attempt to understand the
phenomenon.

Their eventual communication should emerge through repeated exchanges and deliberate
experimentation.

Zeke's developing feelings

His emotional development should be extremely gradual.

A possible progression:

   1.​ Disorientation: Levi is an unfamiliar person who appears to have an intimate
       relationship with the identity Zeke has inherited.
   2.​ Curiosity: Zeke begins observing Levi and the nature of his relationship with Erwin.
   3.​ Recognition: He realizes that their affection is genuine, habitual and deeply
       established.
   4.​ Unexpected attachment: He begins looking forward to interactions that were never
       intended for him.
   5.​ Guilt: He becomes increasingly aware that Levi is directing his affection toward
       someone else.
   6.​ Emotional conflict: He starts developing feelings for Levi as an individual, rather
       than simply enjoying the experience of being loved.
   7.​ Acceptance: He must eventually recognize that Levi's love for Erwin is real and
       independent of his own feelings.

Do not rush these stages.

Zeke should not become a different person simply because he experiences affection.




12. WATCHING ROOM
CHARACTERIZATION
Zeke's participation in the watching room should follow a separate, equally gradual
development.

Initially, he is an enemy surrounded by people who have every reason to distrust and
despise him.

He should understand this.

He is not particularly interested in begging for acceptance.

He may respond to hostility with dry humour, intellectual arguments or deliberate
provocation.

However, he should not constantly antagonize everyone simply to generate conflict.

Early interactions

   ●​ He observes the group before participating.
   ●​ He is particularly interested in Erwin's reactions.
   ●​ He recognizes Levi's hostility and does not underestimate it.
   ●​ He may engage Armin in intellectual discussions.
   ●​ He can exchange sarcastic remarks with Jean and Connie.
   ●​ Hange's scientific curiosity may interest him, although he will not automatically
      cooperate.
   ●​ He should remain wary of Eren, especially given their complicated canon
      relationship.

Developing relationships

Over time, the shared experience of watching the AU can create opportunities for
conversation.
The others may discover aspects of Zeke's personality that contradict their initial
assumptions.

Zeke may also begin to understand the people he previously regarded primarily as enemies.

However:

Understanding is not forgiveness.

His actions in canon remain significant.

The group should not suddenly forget the people who have died because of him.

His eventual integration should emerge from repeated interactions, disagreements, shared
observations and the gradual development of mutual understanding.

Erwin and Zeke

Their relationship should be particularly interesting.

Both are intelligent military strategists, but their motivations and ideologies differ
considerably.

They can recognize each other's intelligence without agreeing with each other's decisions.

Their eventual rapport should be built on intellectual respect, honest disagreements and the
realization that neither is as simple as the other initially assumed.




13. FINAL WRITING PRINCIPLES
When writing Zeke, remember:

   1.​ He is intelligent, not omniscient.
   2.​ He is emotionally guarded, not emotionless.
   3.​ He can be funny without becoming a comic-relief character.
   4.​ He is capable of genuine affection, but intimacy is unfamiliar territory in this AU.
   5.​ He should retain his ideological convictions even when confronted with contradictory
       experiences.
   6.​ He is manipulative when circumstances call for it, but not every interaction needs to
       be a calculated move.
   7.​ He should not be reduced to his relationship with Levi.
   8.​ His relationship with Erwin should develop independently of the romantic storyline.
   9.​ His eventual friendships with the watching-room characters must be earned.
   10.​His moral complexity should remain intact.

The most important principle: Zeke Yeager is a person before he is a redemption arc.
His development should not be about transforming him into a harmless, lovable character.

It should be about allowing a complicated, intelligent, deeply flawed man to encounter
people and experiences that challenge the assumptions upon which he has built his entire
life.
