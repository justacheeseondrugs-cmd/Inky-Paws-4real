# Coriolanus Snow / Edmund Voss

ESTABLISHED: one person, not two personalities. Exact extracted dossier sections II and IX follow. Their example gestures remain examples, not mandatory chapter events. Source: Snow AU, pp. 2–4 and 20–22.

II. CORIOLANUS SNOW:
CHARACTERIZATION
1. The source characterization
Use young Coriolanus Snow from The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, particularly Tom
Blyth's portrayal, as the psychological and behavioural foundation.

Do not write him as the elderly President Snow.

He is young, physically recognizable, intelligent, articulate, ambitious, socially perceptive and
capable of genuine emotional warmth.

His personality contains several seemingly contradictory qualities:

    ●​ He is charming but deeply self-interested.
    ●​ He is affectionate but possessive.
    ●​ He is intelligent enough to understand other people's emotions but not necessarily
       willing to respect them.
    ●​ He is capable of sincere attachment while also rationalizing actions that hurt the
       people he claims to love.
    ●​ He can be generous, funny and considerate.
    ●​ He is intensely concerned with his future, reputation and position.
    ●​ He dislikes appearing vulnerable or powerless.
    ●​ He prefers to control circumstances rather than openly demand control.
    ●​ He can convince himself that morally questionable actions are reasonable if he
       believes the outcome is justified.

He should never be written as a one-dimensional villain.

His feelings for Levi are real.

His tenderness is real.

His capacity to hurt other people is also real.

The story must allow all three things to coexist.


2. His relationship with power
Snow does not necessarily want power simply because he enjoys dominating others.

He associates power with security, independence, status and the ability to prevent himself
from ever becoming vulnerable again.

He dislikes being dependent on people or circumstances he cannot control.

He wants to be someone who can make decisions instead of waiting for others to make
them for him.

This tendency becomes especially important after losing Levi.

He eventually convinces himself that the problem was not his ambition or his methods, but
his previous inability to provide the life Levi deserved.

In other words, he believes that if he had possessed more influence, money, status and
authority, he could have prevented their separation.

He does not initially consider that Levi objected to the choices he made, not merely the
circumstances surrounding them.


3. His emotional intelligence
Snow is observant.

He notices changes in tone, posture, facial expressions, hesitation and word choice.

He remembers details about people and uses this knowledge naturally.
However, his emotional intelligence has a serious limitation: he tends to interpret other
people's feelings through the framework of his own desires.

He may correctly recognize that Levi is angry, hurt or uncomfortable, but conclude that those
feelings are temporary and can be overcome.

He may notice Levi's closeness with Erwin and understand that it matters, while convincing
himself that it cannot possibly outweigh their shared history.

He is capable of understanding what another person wants.

He struggles to accept that what they want might be incompatible with what he wants.


4. His affection
Snow is capable of sincere, wholehearted tenderness.

Do not make every affectionate interaction calculated.

He genuinely enjoys taking care of Levi, making him comfortable, touching him, listening to
him and being near him.

He remembers small details because he was paying attention, not because he was
constructing a strategy.

He can be playful, indulgent, teasing and occasionally almost boyish when genuinely happy.

He likes being loved by Levi. He likes knowing Levi trusts him enough to relax around him.

However, his affection gradually becomes entangled with entitlement.

He believes he knows Levi exceptionally well, and this familiarity contributes to his conviction
that he understands what is best for him.

He sometimes treats their shared history as evidence that he has a privileged claim to Levi's
future.

That is one of his central flaws.

IX. MANNERISMS AND DIALOGUE
GUIDE
1. Physical mannerisms
Use recurring gestures consistently, without repeating them mechanically.

Potential habits:

   ●​   Smooths his cuffs or adjusts his sleeves when thinking.
   ●​   Holds eye contact a little longer than most people.
   ●​   Tilts his head slightly when interested.
   ●​   Uses a small, controlled smile in formal situations.
   ●​   Keeps his posture immaculate.
   ●​   Rarely fidgets openly.
   ●​   Becomes very still when irritated.
    ●​ May tighten his jaw when something genuinely unsettles him.
    ●​ Uses people's names deliberately.
    ●​ Speaks with measured precision.

Around Levi, additional habits may appear:

    ●​   His gaze lingers on Levi's face.
    ●​   He notices small physical details immediately.
    ●​   His expression softens before he can fully control it.
    ●​   He instinctively moves closer.
    ●​   He remembers familiar gestures and routines.
    ●​   His voice may become warmer and less formal.
    ●​   He may smile more naturally.
    ●​   He sometimes forgets that Levi no longer welcomes the familiarity.


2. Dialogue
Snow's dialogue should be intelligent, controlled and natural.

He is not constantly delivering philosophical speeches.

He can be conversational, humorous, teasing and charming.

He understands social situations.

He rarely needs to explain how intelligent he is.

His language should change depending on the person he is addressing.

With political allies:

    ●​   Polite.
    ●​   Measured.
    ●​   Diplomatic.
    ●​   Occasionally disarmingly warm.

With people he dislikes:

    ●​ Courteous but distant.
    ●​ Subtle rather than openly hostile.
    ●​ Capable of delivering criticism without raising his voice.

With Erwin:

    ●​   Intellectually engaged.
    ●​   Respectful when appropriate.
    ●​   Interested in his strategic thinking.
    ●​   Politically careful.
    ●​   Quietly competitive as the story progresses.
With Levi:

   ●​   More personal.
   ●​   More familiar.
   ●​   More openly affectionate.
   ●​   Sometimes teasing.
   ●​   Occasionally caught off guard by the distance Levi maintains.

Avoid making him sound like a generic dark romance villain.


3. Internal monologue
The audience should sometimes see thoughts that contradict his public behaviour.

For example, he might compliment Erwin's political judgement while privately evaluating how
much influence the Commander has over Levi.

He might listen to Levi speak and suddenly remember a mundane evening in their old home.

He might notice Levi's expression and think he remembers exactly how to make him smile.

He might genuinely enjoy a conversation with Erwin while simultaneously considering him a
potential obstacle.

His internal thoughts should not all be about possessing Levi.

They should include genuine memories, ordinary observations, political analysis,
amusement, affection, irritation and self-justification.

Do not make every thought an exposition paragraph.

Use internal monologue selectively.
